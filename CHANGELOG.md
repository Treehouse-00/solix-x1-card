# Changelog

## 1.0.22

- **A new arrangement.** On the left, the period's title bar, the drawing of the home with the line of sources and the battery's ring beside it, and the power chart; on the right, the place's name, the period's six headline figures three to a row, the period's figures (self-sufficient, solar kept, saved) and the battery's charge chart. The drawing of the home takes two thirds of its card and the battery's tile a third, and the card is as tall as the drawing needs to fill its share; that height comes out of the charts' row, which keeps a third of the column so the power chart always carries a scale. The place's two cards grow to end level with it, their tiles taller, so the two charts share a row: the battery's chart starts level with the power chart and is always as tall. The drawing is rooted at its card's left edge where the house runs off it. Reading either chart reads the same moment on both. Under 1000 px the cards stack in one order (the house and its battery, the place and its figures, the period's bar, the power, the battery's charge, the meaning), and each keeps the desktop's grammar: on a tablet the drawing still stands beside the battery's tile and the six figures run six across where they fit; on a phone the tile comes first, the ring and its words in a row under the line of sources, and the drawing under it; the period's views take a row of their own, the download at its right; and the battery's chart is as tall as the power chart at every width.
- **The junction drawn solid.** Every line ends at one point behind the X1's plate, each leg level or plumb, and the plate stands off the wall with a face, a lit top and a shaded side.
- **The ring says only the charge,** its number alone, lighter and set at the ring's optical centre, without a percent sign, a state word or a heading; the line that projected when the battery would empty or fill is gone.
- **A round pole.** The service pole stands just past the roof, behind its edge, and rises to the top of the figures, where it is Grid's pointer: a cylinder shaded across its width, its crossarms beams laid across the line, toned from the house's own palette so it recedes into the card. The grid's line runs straight along the gable into it, and the drawing closes in on the right.
- **One colour per source.** The battery is blue everywhere: the ring, the towers, the lines, the icon and the charts. The grid's violet is a little deeper beside it, and the two are set apart in lightness so they part for colour-blind eyes too. Solar sold is green.
- **A plainer power chart.** One key with each source once (Solar, Battery, Grid, Solar sold), larger and easier to see; the line's halves are named Power demand and Power surplus (Energy in the week, month and year views); the sentence that explained the halves is gone.
- **Figures without state words.** Grid and Battery no longer say idle, buying, selling, charging or discharging; the lines show which way the power goes. Grid stands beside its pole, flush with Battery's right edge, with no lead of its own.
- **The battery icon lies on its side,** rounder, its terminal a pixel clear of the body.
- **One family of icons.** The sun, the battery and the power plant share a centre, a size and a weight, the plant's corners softened like the battery's and lifted off its base. Each is drawn a touch fuller where its colour is faintest (the sun on a light theme, the plant on a dark one), so all three weigh the same on either.
- **Parts set apart by tone.** The battery's panel beside the drawing is a tile of its own, the line of sources its head over a hairline and the ring under it, taking the width the drawing leaves so no empty band opens between them; the six figures are tiles too, and so are the period's three, in the same three columns; every tile carries its name at the top and its figure at the foot, and the period's names are shorter (solar kept, saved). Both carry their own tone, so they stay separate in a theme that draws no card borders.
- **The house lit, not drawn.** Each plane takes the light its facing gets from the high sun at the upper right (the back slope the most, the front slope next, the gable and the long wall only the sky's), and a gradient carries the light through it: the ridge catching the sun, the wall dark under the eave, the feet occluded where they meet the ground, the shadow softening as it falls away. The panels are glass, with a sheen toward the sun; the cabinets and their charge darken toward the floor. No edge is stroked: planes meet by value alone. On a dark theme the house is low in key, its long wall darker than the card; on a light one it is high in key, and in both the lit planes are the lightest. Lines carrying nothing stay a quiet step from the planes they cross.
- **The power chart at full width.** Its name and key share a row over the plot, as the battery's chart does, and the plot takes the card's whole width. A band's name is set in an ink that reads on its band rather than ringed in black, and the note where the cloud's data ends sits above the plot, clear of the bands. A plot too short for a full scale keeps one tick a side.

## 1.0.7

- **Powered, at a glance.** The sentence over the drawing is now one line: an icon for each source feeding the house, in its colour and in the drawing's order (the sun, the battery, a power plant for the grid), then "Powered". The full sentence is its tooltip and its label for screen readers, and old data greys the icons.
- **Panels lit by what they make.** The roof carries eight panels, each a grid of 48 by 48 pixels in the roof's plane. The share lit is the array's output over the most it makes, scattered at random: none at night, all at its best. A pixel lit at a lower power stays lit as the power rises.
- **`array_kw`.** The most the array makes, which sets when every pixel is lit; its best clear-day output is a good figure. Without it the card uses the best five minutes of the last 30 days.
- **Closer on the junction.** The drawing is framed from the panels to the pole, so the house is larger and the junction sits near the middle. Solar runs off the top row of panels and drops straight into the junction, the battery's line runs along the foot of the wall to the towers, and the house's line ends in a plain box on the long wall.
- **Solid and softly lit.** No outlines: the house is drawn in planes under a high sun at the upper right, the roof lightest and the long wall darkest, with short shadows falling to the lower left and each panel standing off the roof. Lines carrying nothing show faintly against the house.
- **Battery towers rooted at the right.** Slimmer cabinets tinted with the battery's colour hold their charge. The rightmost stays put and each added pack pushes the row left, so with fewer packs the battery's line runs farther.
- **One grid for the figures.** Solar, Grid, House and Battery each sit flush against an upright, solid lead, in two rows; dashes are left to the lines that carry power. Battery's name sits over its state, then its figure.
- On a phone, the drawing takes the height its width gives it.

## 1.0.1

- **One lane per source.** A line that carries power from more than one source now carries a thin lane for each, side by side in its own colour: solar and the battery feeding the house together show as amber beside green, where before the battery's colour covered the solar it ran with.
- **Finer beads.** The beads are thinner, with shorter gaps, so each lane reads as one continuous line.

## 1.0.0

The first release.

- **The battery first.** The state of charge in a ring that carries the backup reserve, with what it means under it: when the battery empties or fills at the present rate, or that what is left is kept for outages.
- **The home, drawn.** A line in words over a drawing of the home: the panels, the battery towers filling with the charge, and the circuit through the X1. Each line that carries power takes the colour of the energy on it, with beads drifting the way the power goes.
- **Day, week, month and year.** Anker's own totals as six headline figures, with the change against the previous period; a chart of what ran the house and what was stored or sold; the battery's charge through the day, or each day's self-sufficiency; and the period's self-sufficiency, solar kept and saving, priced at your tariff if you give one.
- **Read any moment.** Hover, drag a finger across a chart, or use the arrow keys, and download any period as CSV.
- **Fits a laptop.** One screen without scrolling, in light and dark, and a phone layout that stacks the same parts.
